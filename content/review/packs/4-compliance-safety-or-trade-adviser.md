## What this is

A learning app for small businesses has a map of everything a business does, and each step (a "stop") has a learning track. These are **drafts** that an AI assistant wrote for steps where the app has little or no teaching material. **Nothing here is live.** Please read each one as someone who does this work, and say whether it is right.

They were written from general UK small-business knowledge, **not from cited documents**, so treat every fact as unchecked. Rules, thresholds and deadlines change, and some are different outside the UK.

## How to review

For each item, read the passage, then the questions. Tick the boxes, and write corrections in the spaces or straight onto the text. You do not need to fix everything; a note such as "wrong: the limit is X" is enough.

- ☐ **Accurate.** Is every statement in the passage true, and current?
- ☐ **UK only?** Would any of it be wrong or misleading outside the UK? Say which part.
- ☐ **Clear.** Would a small-business owner who knows nothing about this follow it?
- ☐ **Questions.** One clearly right answer, supported by the passage? Are the wrong answers plausible rather than silly?
- ☐ **Source.** Write down the real source you checked it against (a GOV.UK page, a regulator's guidance). The app needs one before this goes live.

**Verdict for each item:** Approve / Approve with changes / Reject. A "Reject" with a reason is as useful as an approval.

## 1. Importing and exporting

*Step on the map:* Import & Export  ·  *Learning track:* `delivery-management`  ·  *Id:* `trade`

### The passage

> Moving goods across borders adds steps and costs. Each shipment needs a customs declaration and a commodity code that classifies the goods, which decides the duty rate and any restrictions. You may owe import duty and VAT on arrival, and need a registration number to import or export (in the UK, an EORI number). Incoterms are standard shipping terms that say who pays for transport, insurance and customs at each point, and where risk passes from seller to buyer. Include duty, freight, insurance and fees in your price, or your margin disappears. A freight forwarder or customs broker can handle the paperwork.

## 2. Accessibility for customers

*Step on the map:* Accessibility for Customers  ·  *Learning track:* `gglink-sales-support`  ·  *Id:* `access`

### The passage

> Accessibility means making your premises, website and service usable by everyone, including disabled people. In the UK, businesses that provide services must not discriminate and must make reasonable adjustments, which can be as simple as a ramp, clear signs, large print, quiet times or help on request. For websites, use clear headings, good contrast, text descriptions for images, captions for video, and make everything usable with a keyboard. Ask disabled customers what would help and test with real users. Train staff to offer help without assuming. Good accessibility helps many others too, such as older people and parents with prams.

## 3. Product safety and labels

*Step on the map:* Product Safety & Labels  ·  *Learning track:* `governance-compliance`  ·  *Id:* `prodsafe`

### The passage

> If you make or sell goods, you must sell safe products. Check what rules cover your product: some, such as toys, electrical items and cosmetics, need testing and a mark (in the UK, UKCA, and in some cases CE). Labels must carry the information the law requires, which can include the business name and address, usage instructions and warnings, quantity, batch number, and for food the ingredients with allergens highlighted. Keep test results and supplier records. If you learn a product is unsafe, stop selling it and tell customers and the authorities as required, and have a recall plan. Importers and sellers share responsibility with makers.

## 4. Licences and permits

*Step on the map:* Licences & Permits  ·  *Learning track:* `governance-compliance`  ·  *Id:* `licence`

### The passage

> Some activities need permission before you start: selling alcohol, serving hot food late at night, street trading, running a taxi, carrying waste, playing recorded music in public, and more. The rules and the issuing body, often the local authority or a sector regulator, depend on what you do and where. Trading without a required licence can be an offence, and an application can take weeks, so find out early, before you sign a lease or print a menu. Keep a list of each licence, its conditions and its renewal date, and display it if required.

## 5. Health and safety at work

*Step on the map:* Health & Safety  ·  *Learning track:* `risk-resilience-leadership`  ·  *Id:* `safety`

### The passage

> Employers have a legal duty to look after the health and safety of staff and others affected by their work. The core is the risk assessment: find what could cause harm, decide who might be harmed and how, decide what controls are needed, record the findings if you have five or more employees, and review them. Provide training, protective equipment where needed, first aid, and a safe place to work. Report serious accidents and diseases as the law requires. Involve staff: they often know the real dangers. Safety is not a one-off task; keep checking.
