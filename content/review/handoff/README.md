# Sending the review packs to reviewers

The 60 drafted passages and 240 questions in `../business-stops/` are **not live**. They need a person who does the work to check them first. This page has a cover note for each of the six packs, ready to paste into an email, and the steps for what to do with what comes back. Keep track of who has what in [tracker.md](tracker.md).

## Decide these before you send anything

I cannot decide these for you, and a reviewer will ask. Fill in the square brackets in the notes below once you have.

1. **Who is reviewing which pack.** Each pack is written for one kind of person (see the notes). Several packs are small enough for one reviewer; the two practitioner packs can be one person or two.
2. **Credit.** Will you name reviewers (in the app, on the stops they checked, or on a page of acknowledgements), or keep them anonymous? Ask them; do not assume.
3. **Payment or thanks.** Is this paid work, a favour, or an exchange? Say so in the note.
4. **What their review means.** These are teaching drafts for a learning app, not professional advice. If a reviewer is cautious about their name or professional body being attached, say clearly what you will and will not do with their review. If you want a stronger sign-off, that is a separate conversation, and probably one to have with whoever advises you on liability.
5. **A return date.** The notes have a `[date]` placeholder.

## What a reviewer is being asked to do

The same in every pack, in plain words: read each short passage (about 120 words) and its four multiple-choice questions, tick the boxes, **correct anything wrong or out of date**, say whether any of it is untrue outside the UK, give a verdict (approve, approve with changes, or reject), and **name the real source they checked it against**, such as a GOV.UK page or a regulator's guidance. A rejection with a reason is as useful as an approval.

My time estimates are about six minutes an item. They are guesses, not measurements, so ask the first reviewer how long it really took and adjust the rest.

## How to send a pack

The packs are Markdown files in `../packs/`. They read fine in any text editor, and GitHub shows them as formatted pages if the reviewer can open the repository. Most reviewers will want Word or PDF; there is no converter in this project, so paste the pack into Word or print it from a Markdown viewer. Offer a shared document they can comment on if they prefer.

## When feedback comes back

1. Record it in [tracker.md](tracker.md) (who, when, verdict counts).
2. Apply the corrections to the matching file in `../business-stops/` (the JSON is the source of truth; the packs are generated from it). Replace each item's `source` with the real source the reviewer named. For a rejected item, delete it, or rewrite it and send it back.
3. `npm run check:review` (checks structure and that the packs are not stale), then `npm run review:packs` to regenerate the packs.
4. Once a file is approved, import it. Because it says `"status": "review"`, its questions are saved **unpublished**:
   ```bash
   npm run import:knowledge -- content/review/business-stops/people-operations.json
   ```
   Try it against a copy of the data first if you want to see what it does. An admin then activates the questions in the question bank, and only then do they enter exams.
5. Move approved files out of `content/review/`, and run `npm run report:coverage -- --write` so the stop cards show the new coverage.

Do not import anything a reviewer rejected, or anything with no source recorded.

## What reviewing does and does not do

Four approved questions per stop is a start, not a fix: a stop needs 15 matching questions to stop being "thin" on its card. Reviewing makes the drafts safe to use; it does not make the stops well covered.
